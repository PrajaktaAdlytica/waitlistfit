# WaitListFit — full homepage transition review

Result: reviewed and polished in the local preview.

The supplied Dawn recording uses persistent light, a recurring curve, curved surface edges and restrained object movement to connect its chapters. WaitListFit now follows those continuity principles while retaining its approved cream-and-gold palette, departure-board hero, operational content and concept boundaries. This is a motion-direction comparison, not a claim of pixel equivalence with Dawn.

## What changed

1. **The route crossed reading areas.** Desktop evidence and mobile people/evidence captures showed the line running through headings. It now stays in the page gutters and changes sides in the empty handoffs.
2. **Mobile entrances could clip controls and copy.** Phone module cards now enter vertically, with no sideways rotation. All sampled visible cards stayed horizontally in bounds.
3. **The opening left the phone frame too early.** The route's usable range is measured against the actual card width and viewport. The card remains on the curve and in bounds. Nearby decorative labels fade only when the card needs their space.
4. **Long panel crossfades mixed text layers.** Outgoing panels now finish their short exit before incoming text appears. The panels share a consistent frame; at 775 × 804 each measured 654px high. No sample in the desktop sweep had two panel opacities above 0.12 together.
5. **Some text was too faint during reveals.** Constraint values, FAQ rows, workflow labels and role tags remain opaque; the lines and restrained movement carry the animation.
6. **Scroll and pointer follow needed steadier timing.** The light field uses a short, frame-rate-independent follow. Layout measurements are cached and reads precede presentation writes. This is an implementation improvement, not a measured FPS claim.
7. **Mobile viewport changes could disturb scene progress.** The story uses a stable `100svh` reference, and the replay pin follows the same stable-height rule. The 775 × 720 window correctly uses ordinary flow; 775 × 804 retains the bounded week.
8. **Reverse scrolling restarted the board unnecessarily.** The automatic flip plays once per mount, with explicit replay available. Clock digits flip from blank to their actual values rather than cycling through invalid intermediate times. The visual leaves are hidden from accessibility navigation and the board has a complete labelled group.

## Checks and limits

- Desktop 1440 × 900; mobile 390 × 844; common user window 775 × 804; short window 775 × 720. Browser captures use density 1. Both full forward sweeps and reverse sweeps encountered all 13 main sections; the footer was inspected separately.
- No horizontal document overflow in the sweeps. The mobile opening stayed horizontally in bounds and no sampled visible module card extended outside the viewport.
- Motion-off removes pinning and spatial animation while retaining the evidence. Label opacity and the background's stopped animation were checked. OS reduced-motion handling is implemented; the host OS preference was not changed.
- The motion control's hit area was increased. A DOM hit-test just outside its visible circle still resolved to the motion button.
- The complete scroll leaves the product state pending. Explicit approval creates only a local handoff; reset reverses it. No appointment is booked or outreach sent.
- The five source offers, source/rationale/owner context, uncertainty, hypothetical price, baseline and guardrails, 8/2/1 gate and stop rule remain. No routes, live systems, analytics, data collection or deployment were added.
- Browser error/warning logs were empty in the checked interactions. The production build and all four retained worker/package tests pass.
- This is desktop-browser viewport testing, not physical-device, full assistive-technology or low-end GPU certification. Hidden-page animation pausing is implemented, but was not tested by changing host visibility state. Screenshot sequences do not constitute a frame-time benchmark.
- Dawn's source is the user-supplied screen recording, sampled at 0–60 seconds. No claim is made about the current live Dawn website.
